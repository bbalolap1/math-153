## Q.1

Given \[ f(x)=2x\^2+bx+c \] with vertex \[ (-1,-1). \]

Vertex form is \[ f(x)=a(x-h)\^2+k. \]

Here \[ a=2,`\qquad `{=tex}h=-1,`\qquad `{=tex}k=-1. \]

Therefore \[ f(x)=2(x+1)\^2-1 \] \[ =2(x\^2+2x+1)-1 \] \[ =2x\^2+4x+1.
\]

Comparing with \[ 2x\^2+bx+c, \] we obtain \[
`\boxed{b=4,\qquad c=1}`{=tex}. \]

## Q.2

The equation of a circle with center ((h,k)) and radius (r) is \[
(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r\^2. \]

For center ((4,2)) and radius (2): \[ (x-4)^2+(y-2)^2=4. \]

At an (x)-intercept, \[ y=0. \]

Thus \[ (x-4)^2+(0-2)^2=4 \] \[ (x-4)\^2+4=4 \] \[ (x-4)\^2=0 \] \[ x=4.
\]

So the circle has one (x)-intercept: \[ `\boxed{(4,0)}`{=tex}. \]

## Q.3

\[ `\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}`{=tex} = `\frac{x^2-2x+1}{x-1}`{=tex}. \]

The denominator cannot equal zero: \[ x-1`\ne0`{=tex} \] so \[
`\boxed{x\ne1}`{=tex}. \]

Factor the numerator: \[ x^2-2x+1=(x-1)^2. \]

Then, over the original domain, \[ `\frac{(x-1)^2}{x-1}`{=tex}=x-1. \]

Therefore \[ `\boxed{\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=x-1,\qquad x\ne1}`{=tex}. \]

## Q.4

\[ (f`\circ `{=tex}g)(x)=f(g(x)). \]

Since \[ g(x)=2x+4, \] we get \[ (f`\circ `{=tex}g)(x) = f(2x+4) =
`\sqrt{2x+4}`{=tex}. \]

For a real square root: \[ 2x+4`\ge0`{=tex} \] \[ x`\ge-2`{=tex}. \]

Therefore \[ `\boxed{(f\circ g)(x)=\sqrt{2x+4}}`{=tex} \] with domain \[
`\boxed{[-2,\infty)}`{=tex}. \]
