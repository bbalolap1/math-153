# 3.2 --- Graphs of Equations

## Page 1 --- Graphs of Equations

Sketch the graph of \[ y=2x-1 \]

  \(x\)     -1    0   1   2
  ------- ---- ---- --- ---
  \(y\)     -3   -1   1   3

The graph is the straight line through ((-1,-3),(0,-1),(1,1),(2,3)).

## Page 2 --- Parabola

Sketch \[ y=x\^2-3. \]

  \(x\)     -3   -2    0   2   3
  ------- ---- ---- ---- --- ---
  \(y\)      6    1   -3   1   6

Vertex: \[ `\boxed{(0,-3)}`{=tex} \]

For \[ y=ax\^2+bx+c, \] if (a\>0), the parabola opens up and the vertex
is a minimum; if (a\<0), it opens down and the vertex is a maximum.

## Page 3 --- Intercepts of (y=x\^2-3)

For (x)-intercepts, set (y=0): \[ x\^2-3=0 \] \[
x=`\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt3`{=tex} \] so \[
`\boxed{(\sqrt3,0),\;(-\sqrt3,0)}`{=tex}. \]

For the (y)-intercept, set (x=0): \[ `\boxed{(0,-3)}`{=tex}. \]

## Page 4 --- Symmetry

-   (y)-axis symmetry: substitute (-x) for (x).
-   (x)-axis symmetry: substitute (-y) for (y).
-   Origin symmetry: substitute both (-x) for (x) and (-y) for (y).

Example: \[ 4y=x\^3. \] Then \[
4(-y)=(-x)^3`\Rightarrow `{=tex}-4y=-x^3`\Rightarrow`{=tex}4y=x\^3. \]

For \[ x=y\^2, \] replacing (y) by (-y) gives \[ x=(-y)^2=y^2. \]

## Page 5 --- Sideways Parabola

\[ y\^2=x \]

  ------- ---- ---- --- --- ---
  \(y\)     -2   -1   0   1   2

Vertex: \[ `\boxed{(0,0)}`{=tex} \]

Solving for (y): \[ `\boxed{y=\pm\sqrt{x}}`{=tex}. \]

## Page 7 --- Equation of a Circle

For center \[ C(h,k) \] and point \[ P(x,y) \] on a circle of radius
(r), \[ d(P,C)=r. \]

Thus \[ `\sqrt{(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2}`{=tex}=r \] and \[
`\boxed{(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2}`{=tex}. \]

## Page 8 --- Circle from Center and Point

Center: \[ C(-2,3) \] Point: \[ D(4,5) \]

Radius: \[ r=`\sqrt{(4-(-2))^2+(5-3)^2}`{=tex} =`\sqrt{40}`{=tex}. \]

Therefore \[ `\boxed{(x+2)^2+(y-3)^2=40}`{=tex}. \]

## Page 9 --- Center and Radius by Completing the Square

\[ 3x^2+3y^2-12x+18y=9 \]

Divide by (3): \[ x^2+y^2-4x+6y=3. \]

Complete the square: \[ `\boxed{(x-2)^2+(y+3)^2=16}`{=tex}. \]

Center: \[ `\boxed{(2,-3)}`{=tex} \]

Radius: \[ `\boxed{4}`{=tex} \]

## Page 10 --- Upper Semicircle

Circle centered at ((0,0)) with radius (3): \[ x^2+y^2=9. \]

Solve for (y): \[ y=`\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt{9-x^2}`{=tex}. \]

Upper semicircle: \[ `\boxed{y=\sqrt{9-x^2}}`{=tex}. \]

Using both signs gives the full circle. The upper semicircle passes the
vertical line test.

## Page 11 --- Left Semicircle

From \[ x^2+y^2=9, \] solve for (x): \[
x=`\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt{9-y^2}`{=tex}. \]

Left half: \[ `\boxed{x=-\sqrt{9-y^2}}`{=tex}. \]

It fails the vertical line test, so it cannot be expressed as (y=f(x)),
but it can be expressed as (x=f(y)).
