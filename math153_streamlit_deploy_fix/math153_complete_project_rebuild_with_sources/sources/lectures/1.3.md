# Polynomials and Factoring

### Variables

Examples:

\[ x, y, z, a, b, c, r, s, t, `\text{etc.}`{=tex} \]

### Constants

Examples:

\[
-2, 0, `\pi`{=tex}, `\sqrt[3]{5}`{=tex}, `\frac72`{=tex}, `\text{etc.}`{=tex}
\]

A **term** is a product of constants and variables, usually written in a
simplified form.

### Example

\[
(-3)`\left`{=tex}(-`\frac17`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})`\cdot `{=tex}x`\cdot `{=tex}y`\cdot `{=tex}x
= `\frac`{=tex}37x\^2y \]

A **polynomial** is a sum of terms.

-   1 term = **monomial**
-   2 terms = **binomial**
-   3 terms = **trinomial**
-   (`\ldots`{=tex})

We will focus mostly on polynomials in only one variable.

# Addition / Subtraction

### Addition Example

\[ (x^3+2x^2-5x+7)+(4x^3-5x^2+3) \]

Combine like terms:

\[ =5x^3+(-3)x^2-5x+10 \]

Therefore:

\[ =5x^3-3x^2-5x+10 \]

## Page 3 --- Subtraction and Multiplication

### Subtraction Example

\[ (x^3+2x^2-5x+7)-(4x^3-5x^2+3) \]

Distribute the negative sign:

\[ =(x^3+2x^2-5x+7)+(-4x^3+5x^2-3) \]

Combine like terms:

\[ =-3x^3+7x^2-5x+4 \]

# Multiplication

### Example

\[ (x^2+5x-4)(2x^3+3x-1) \]

Distribute each term:

\[ =x^2(2x^3+3x-1) +5x(2x\^3+3x-1) -4(2x\^3+3x-1) \]

Expand:

\[ =2x^5+3x^3-x^2+10x^4+15x^2-5x-8x^3-12x+4 \]

Combine like terms:

\[ =2x^5+10x^4-5x^3+14x^2-17x+4 \]

## Page 4 --- Factoring Identities

# Factoring Identities

### Difference of Squares

\[ x^2-y^2=(x+y)(x-y) \]

### Square of a Binomial

\[ (x`\pm `{=tex}y)^2=x^2`\pm`{=tex}2xy+y\^2 \]

### Cube of a Binomial

\[ (x`\pm `{=tex}y)\^3 = x^3`\pm`{=tex}3x^2y+3xy^2`\pm `{=tex}y^3 \]

### Sum of Cubes

\[ x^3+y^3 = (x+y)(x^2-xy+y^2) \]

### Difference of Cubes

\[ x^3-y^3 = (x-y)(x^2+xy+y^2) \]

### Example

Factor:

\[ 16a^3+2b^3 \]

First factor out (2):

\[ =2(8a^3+b^3) \]

Recognize:

\[ 8a^3=2^3a^3=(2a)^3 \]

Then use the sum-of-cubes identity:

\[ =2(2a+b)(4a^2-2ab+b^2) \]

## Page 5 --- Factoring Examples

Continue:

\[ x^4-4y^2 \]

Recognize both terms as squares:

\[ x^4=(x^2)\^2 \]

and

\[ 4y^2=(2y)^2 \]

Therefore:

\[ =(x^2)^2-(2y)\^2 \]

Using difference of squares:

\[ =(x^2-2y)(x^2+2y) \]

### Example

Factor:

\[ 4x^5y-9x^3y\^3 \]

Factor out the common factor:

\[ =x^3(4x^2y-9y\^3) \]

Then factor out (y):

\[ =x^3y(4x^2-9y\^2) \]

Recognize a difference of squares:

\[ =x\^3y`\left[(2x)^2-(3y)^2\right]`{=tex}\]

Therefore:

\[ =x\^3y(2x-3y)(2x+3y) \]

## Page 6 --- Factoring by Grouping

# Factoring by Grouping

This can be thought of as a reverse of the distributive property /
**FOIL**.

\[ ac+ad+bc+bd \]

Group the first two and last two terms:

\[ =a(c+d)+b(c+d) \]

Then factor out the common binomial:

\[ =(a+b)(c+d) \]

### Example

Factor:

\[ 3x^3+2x^2-12x-8 \]

Group terms:

\[ =x\^2(3x+2)-4(3x+2) \]

Factor out the common binomial:

\[ =(x\^2-4)(3x+2) \]

Use difference of squares:

\[ =(x-2)(x+2)(3x+2) \]
