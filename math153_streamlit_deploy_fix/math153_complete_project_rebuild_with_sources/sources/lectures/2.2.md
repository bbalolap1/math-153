## Page 1 --- Averages

Before a final exam, a student has test scores:

\[ 72, 80, 65, 78, 60 \]

If the final exam counts as (`\frac13`{=tex}) of the total grade, what
score must the student receive on the final for a total grade of (76)?

The average of the existing test scores is

\[ 71. \]

The test scores count for (`\frac23`{=tex}), while the final counts for
(`\frac13`{=tex}).

Let the final-exam score be (x):

\[ `\frac23`{=tex}(71)+`\frac`{=tex}13x=76 \]

Multiply by (3):

\[ 142+x=228 \]

\[ `\boxed{x=86}`{=tex} \]

## Page 2 --- Discounts / Markups

A clothing store advertises a **20% discount** on all prices. If a shirt
is on sale for (\$30), what was its pre-sale price?

Let (x) be the pre-sale price.

Sale price:

\[ x-(20%`\text{ of }`{=tex}x) \]

\[ =x-0.2x \]

\[ =(1-0.2)x \]

\[ =0.8x \]

Set the sale price equal to \$30:

\[ 30=0.8x \]

\[ x=`\frac{30}{0.8}`{=tex} \]

\[ `\boxed{x=\$37.50}`{=tex} \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 3 --- Simple Interest

An investment firm has (\$100{,}000) to invest for a client and divides
it between stocks (A) and (B).

-   Stock (A): expected annual simple interest (15%)
-   No more than (\$50{,}000) may be invested in stock (A)
-   Stock (B): annual simple interest (10%)

Determine whether the money can be invested so the annual interest is:

-   (a) (\$12{,}000)
-   (b) (\$13{,}000)

Maximum possible interest occurs by putting (\$50{,}000) into each:

\[ I_A=50{,}000(0.15)(1)=7{,}500 \]

\[ I_B=50{,}000(0.10)(1)=5{,}000 \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 4 --- Solving the Investment Problem

Best-case scenario:

\[ I_A=7{,}500,`\qquad `{=tex}I_B=5{,}000 \]

\[ I_A+I_B=12{,}500 \]

Therefore (\$13{,}000) is not possible.

To generate (\$12{,}000), let

\[ x=`\text{investment in stock A}`{=tex}. \]

Then

\[ I_A=0.15x \]

and

\[ I_B=(100{,}000-x)(0.10) \]

\[ =10{,}000-0.10x \]

Total interest:

\[ I_A+I_B = 0.15x+(10{,}000-0.10x) \]

\[ =0.05x+10{,}000 \]

Set this equal to (\$12{,}000):

\[ 0.05x+10{,}000=12{,}000 \]

\[ 0.05x=2{,}000 \]

\[ `\boxed{x=40{,}000}`{=tex} \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 5 --- Mixtures

A pharmacist must prepare **15 mL** of special eye drops for a glaucoma
patient. The eye drops must contain **2% active ingredient**, but the
pharmacist only has **10%** and **1%** solutions.

How much of each solution should be used?

Required active ingredient:

\[ 2%`\text{ of }`{=tex}15`\text{ mL}`{=tex} = 15(0.02) =
0.3`\text{ mL}`{=tex} \]

Let:

\[ x=`\text{mL of 10\% solution}`{=tex} \]

\[ y=`\text{mL of 1\% solution}`{=tex} \]

Active ingredient equation:

\[ 0.10x+0.01y=0.3 \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 6 --- Solving the Mixture

Total volume:

\[ x+y=15 \]

Solve for (x):

\[ x=15-y \]

Substitute:

\[ 0.10(15-y)+0.01y=0.3 \]

\[ 1.5-0.10y+0.01y=0.3 \]

\[ -0.09y=-1.2 \]

\[ `\boxed{y\approx13.3\text{ mL}}`{=tex} \]

Then:

\[ x=15-y \]

\[ x`\approx15`{=tex}-13.3 \]

\[ `\boxed{x\approx1.7\text{ mL}}`{=tex} \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 7 --- Distance, Speed, and Time

A boy can row a boat at a constant rate of **5 mi/hr in still water**.
He rows upstream for **15 minutes** and then rows downstream, returning
to his starting point in **12 minutes**.

Find:

1.  the rate of the current;
2.  the total distance traveled.

Basic formulas:

\[ d=vt \]

\[ v=`\frac `{=tex}dt \]

Convert 15 minutes to hours:

\[ 1`\text{ hr}`{=tex}=60`\text{ min}`{=tex} \]

\[ 15`\text{ min}`{=tex} =
15`\left`{=tex}(`\frac1{60}`{=tex}`\text{ hr}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex}) =
`\frac{15}{60}`{=tex}`\text{ hr}`{=tex} = 0.25`\text{ hr}`{=tex} \]

The answers written in the notes are:

\[ `\boxed{\text{current rate}\approx0.56\text{ mi/hr}}`{=tex} \]

\[ `\boxed{\text{total distance}\approx2.22\text{ mi}}`{=tex} \]
