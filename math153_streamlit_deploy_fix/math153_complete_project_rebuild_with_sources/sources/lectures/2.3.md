## Page 1 --- Definition and Three Methods

A quadratic equation in the variable (x) has the form

\[ ax\^2+bx+c=0 \]

where (a,b,c) are constants and

\[ a`\ne0`{=tex}. \]

### Three methods for solving quadratic equations

1.  Factoring
2.  Completing the square
3.  Quadratic formula

The notes mark completing the square and the quadratic formula as
methods that **always work**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 2 --- Factoring

Factoring relies on the **zero-factor theorem**:

If

\[ ab=0, \]

then

\[ a=0`\quad`{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex}`\quad `{=tex}b=0 \]

(or both).

### Example

\[ 3x\^2=10-x \]

Put into standard form:

\[ 3x\^2+x-10=0 \]

Split the middle term:

\[ 3x\^2-5x+6x-10=0 \]

Group:

\[ x(3x-5)+2(3x-5)=0 \]

Factor:

\[ (x+2)(3x-5)=0 \]

Therefore:

\[ x+2=0 `\quad`{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex}`\quad`{=tex} 3x-5=0 \]

\[ `\boxed{x=-2\quad\text{or}\quad x=\frac53}`{=tex} \]

## Page 4 --- Completing the Square

We know how to solve equations such as

\[ `\sqrt{x^2}`{=tex}=`\sqrt5`{=tex}. \]

Because

\[ `\sqrt{x^2}`{=tex}=\|x\|, \]

we obtain

\[ \|x\|=`\sqrt5`{=tex} \]

and therefore

\[ `\boxed{x=\pm\sqrt5}`{=tex}. \]

Similarly,

\[ `\sqrt{(x+3)^2}`{=tex}=`\sqrt5`{=tex} \]

gives

\[ x+3=`\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt5`{=tex} \]

so

\[ `\boxed{x=-3\pm\sqrt5}`{=tex}. \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 5 --- General Completing-the-Square Technique

Start with

\[ ax\^2+bx+c=0,`\qquad `{=tex}a`\ne0`{=tex}. \]

Divide by (a):

\[ x\^2+`\frac `{=tex}ba x+`\frac `{=tex}ca=0 \]

Complete the square:

\[ x\^2+`\frac `{=tex}ba x+`\frac{b^2}{4a^2}`{=tex}+`\frac `{=tex}ca =
`\frac{b^2}{4a^2}`{=tex} \]

Recognize:

\[ x\^2+2x`\left`{=tex}(`\frac `{=tex}b{2a}`\right`{=tex})
+`\left`{=tex}(`\frac `{=tex}b{2a}`\right`{=tex})\^2 =
`\left`{=tex}(x+`\frac `{=tex}b{2a}`\right`{=tex})\^2 \]

Therefore,

\[
`\left`{=tex}(x+`\frac `{=tex}b{2a}`\right`{=tex})\^2+`\frac `{=tex}ca =
`\frac{b^2}{4a^2}`{=tex} \]

and

\[ `\left`{=tex}(x+`\frac `{=tex}b{2a}`\right`{=tex})\^2 =
`\frac{b^2}{4a^2}`{=tex}-`\frac `{=tex}ca. \]

## Page 6 --- Finding the Missing Term

What value of (d) makes an expression a completed square?

### Example

\[ x^2-3x+d=(x+c)^2 \]

Expand:

\[ x^2-3x+d=x^2+2cx+c\^2 \]

Compare coefficients:

\[ -3=2c \]

\[ c=-`\frac32`{=tex} \]

Then

\[ d=c\^2 \]

\[ `\boxed{d=\left(-\frac32\right)^2=\frac94}`{=tex} \]

### Example

\[ x^2+dx+64=(x+c)^2 \]

Since

\[ 64=c\^2, \]

\[ c=`\pm8`{=tex}. \]

Because

\[ d=2c, \]

the notes obtain

\[ d=16`\quad`{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex}`\quad `{=tex}d=-16. \]

## Pages 7--8 --- Solve by Completing the Square

Solve:

\[ x\^2-5x+3=0 \]

To complete the square, use

\[
d=`\left`{=tex}(-`\frac52`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^2=`\frac{25}{4}`{=tex}.
\]

Add the needed term:

\[ `\left[x^2-5x+\frac{25}{4}\right]`{=tex}+3 = `\frac{25}{4}`{=tex} \]

\[ `\left`{=tex}(x-`\frac52`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^2+3 =
`\frac{25}{4}`{=tex} \]

\[ `\left`{=tex}(x-`\frac52`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^2 =
`\frac{13}{4}`{=tex} \]

Take square roots:

\[ x-`\frac52`{=tex} = `\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt{\frac{13}{4}}`{=tex} =
`\pm`{=tex}`\frac{\sqrt{13}}`{=tex}2 \]

Therefore,

\[ x = `\frac52`{=tex}`\pm`{=tex}`\frac{\sqrt{13}}`{=tex}2 \]

\[ `\boxed{x=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{13}}2}`{=tex} \]

## Page 9 --- Deriving the Quadratic Formula

Return to the general completed-square form:

\[ ax\^2+bx+c=0 \]

\[ `\left`{=tex}(x+`\frac `{=tex}b{2a}`\right`{=tex})\^2 =
`\frac{b^2}{4a^2}`{=tex}-`\frac `{=tex}ca \]

Use a common denominator:

\[ `\left`{=tex}(x+`\frac `{=tex}b{2a}`\right`{=tex})\^2 =
`\frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}`{=tex} \]

Take square roots:

\[ x+`\frac `{=tex}b{2a} =
`\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt{\frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}}`{=tex} \]

# \[

```{=tex}
\frac{\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
```
\]

Therefore,

\[ `\boxed{
x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
}`{=tex} \]

The quantity

\[ `\boxed{b^2-4ac}`{=tex} \]

is the **discriminant**.

## Page 10 --- Discriminant and Nature of the Roots

  Discriminant   Nature of roots
  -------------- --------------------------------------------
  Positive       Two distinct real roots
  Zero           One real root
  Negative       Two distinct complex roots / no real roots

### Example

For

\[ 9x\^2-30x+25=0, \]

\[ a=9,`\qquad `{=tex}b=-30,`\qquad `{=tex}c=25. \]

Use the quadratic formula:

\[ x = `\frac{30\pm\sqrt{(-30)^2-4(9)(25)}}{18}`{=tex} \]

# \[

```{=tex}
\frac{30\pm\sqrt0}{18}
```
\]

# \[

```{=tex}
\frac{30}{18}
```
\]

\[ `\boxed{x=\frac{15}{9}}`{=tex} \]

## Page 11 --- Solving for (x) in Terms of (y)

Given

\[ y=x\^2-6x+5, \]

solve for (x).

Put into standard form:

\[ x\^2-6x+(5-y)=0 \]

so

\[ a=1,`\qquad `{=tex}b=-6,`\qquad `{=tex}c=5-y. \]

Quadratic formula:

\[ x = `\frac{6\pm\sqrt{36-4(5-y)}}`{=tex}2 \]

# \[

`\frac{6\pm\sqrt{4(9-(5-y))}}`{=tex}2 \]

# \[

`\frac{6\pm2\sqrt{9-(5-y)}}`{=tex}2 \]

# \[

3`\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt{9-(5-y)}`{=tex} \]

Since

\[ 9-(5-y)=4+y, \]

\[ `\boxed{x=3\pm\sqrt{4+y}}`{=tex} \]
