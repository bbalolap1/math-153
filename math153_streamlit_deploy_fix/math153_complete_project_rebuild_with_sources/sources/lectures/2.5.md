## Page 1 --- Absolute Value Equations

### Example

\[ 3\|x+1\|-2=-11 \]

Add (2):

\[ 3\|x+1\|=-9 \]

Divide by (3):

\[ \|x+1\|=-3 \]

There are **no solutions**, because absolute value is never negative.

The notes contrast this with:

\[ \|x+1\|=3 \]

which gives

\[ x+1=3 `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}x+1=-3 \]

\[ x=2 `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}x=-4. \]

## Page 2 --- Rational Exponent Equations

### Example

\[ y\^{3/2}=5y \]

Rearrange so one side is zero:

\[ y\^{3/2}-5y=0 \]

Factor:

\[ y(y\^{1/2}-5)=0 \]

Therefore either

\[ y=0 \]

or

\[ y\^{1/2}-5=0. \]

For the second case:

\[ y\^{1/2}=5 \]

\[ (y^{1/2})^2=5\^2 \]

\[ `\boxed{y=25}`{=tex} \]

So the solutions are

\[ `\boxed{y=0,\ 25}`{=tex} \]

### Example

\[ y\^{4/3}=-3y \]

Move everything to one side:

\[ y\^{4/3}+3y=0 \]

Factor:

\[ y(y\^{1/3}+3)=0 \]

Therefore:

\[ y=0 \]

or

\[ y\^{1/3}+3=0. \]

Then

\[ y\^{1/3}=-3 \]

\[ (y^{1/3})^3=(-3)\^3 \]

\[ `\boxed{y=-27}`{=tex} \]

Thus:

\[ `\boxed{y=0,\ -27}`{=tex} \]

## Page 3 --- Quadratic-Type Equations and Substitution

### Example

\[ 2x^4-10x^2+8=0 \]

Factor out (2):

\[ 2(x^4-5x^2+4)=0 \]

Notice:

\[ x^4=(x^2)\^2 \]

Use the substitution

\[ u=x\^2. \]

Then:

\[ 2(u\^2-5u+4)=0 \]

Divide by (2):

\[ u\^2-5u+4=0 \]

Factor:

\[ u\^2-u-4u+4=0 \]

\[ u(u-1)-4(u-1)=0 \]

\[ (u-4)(u-1)=0 \]

Therefore:

\[ u=4 `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}u=1. \]

Substitute back (u=x\^2):

\[ x\^2=4 `\Rightarrow `{=tex}x=`\pm2`{=tex} \]

\[ x\^2=1 `\Rightarrow `{=tex}x=`\pm1`{=tex} \]

Final solutions:

\[ `\boxed{x=\pm1,\ \pm2}`{=tex} \]

## Page 4 --- Another Substitution Example

### Example

\[ 3x^{2/3}+4x^{1/3}-4=0 \]

Let

\[ u=x\^{1/3}. \]

Then

\[ x^{2/3}=(x^{1/3})^2=u^2. \]

So:

\[ 3u\^2+4u-4=0 \]

Split the middle term:

\[ 3u\^2+6u-2u-4=0 \]

Group:

\[ 3u(u+2)-2(u+2)=0 \]

\[ (3u-2)(u+2)=0 \]

Thus:

\[ u=`\frac23`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}u=-2.
\]

Since

\[ u=x\^{1/3}, \]

then

\[ x=u\^3. \]

Therefore:

\[ x=`\left`{=tex}(`\frac23`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^3=`\frac8{27}`{=tex}
\]

or

\[ x=(-2)\^3=-8. \]

\[ `\boxed{x=\frac8{27}\text{ or }-8}`{=tex} \]

## Page 5 --- Substitution with a Rational Expression

### Example

\[ `\left`{=tex}(`\frac{x}{x-2}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^2
-2`\left`{=tex}(`\frac{x}{x-2}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex}) -15=0 \]

Let

\[ u=`\frac{x}{x-2}`{=tex}. \]

Then:

\[ u\^2-2u-15=0 \]

Factor:

\[ (u-5)(u+3)=0 \]

So:

\[ u=5 `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}u=-3. \]

### Case 1

\[ `\frac{x}{x-2}`{=tex}=5 \]

\[ x=5(x-2) \]

\[ x=5x-10 \]

\[ -4x=-10 \]

\[ `\boxed{x=\frac52}`{=tex} \]

### Case 2

\[ `\frac{x}{x-2}`{=tex}=-3 \]

\[ x=-3(x-2) \]

\[ x=-3x+6 \]

\[ 4x=6 \]

\[ `\boxed{x=\frac32}`{=tex} \]

## Page 7 --- Radical Equation Requiring Repeated Squaring

### Example

\[ `\sqrt{2x-3}`{=tex}-`\sqrt{x+7}`{=tex}+2=0 \]

Rearrange:

\[ `\sqrt{2x-3}`{=tex}+2=`\sqrt{x+7}`{=tex} \]

Square both sides:

\[ (`\sqrt{2x-3}`{=tex}+2)^2=(`\sqrt{x+7}`{=tex})^2 \]

Expand:

\[ (2x-3)+4`\sqrt{2x-3}`{=tex}+4=x+7 \]

Rearrange:

\[ 4`\sqrt{2x-3}`{=tex}=-x+6 \]

Square again:

\[ 4^2(2x-3)=(-x+6)^2 \]

\[ 16(2x-3)=x\^2-12x+36 \]

Move everything to one side:

\[ x\^2-44x+84=0 \]

Quadratic formula:

\[ x=`\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}`{=tex} \]

\[ x=`\frac{44\pm\sqrt{44^2-4(84)}}`{=tex}2 \]

\[ =`\frac{44\pm40}{2}`{=tex} \]

This produces the candidates

\[ x=42 `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}x=2. \]

The notes cross out (42), leaving

\[ `\boxed{x=2}`{=tex} \]

after checking against the original radical equation.
