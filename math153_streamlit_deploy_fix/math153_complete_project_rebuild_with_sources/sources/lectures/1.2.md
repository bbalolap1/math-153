## Page 1 --- Exponents and Radicals

# Exponents and Radicals

An exponent represents repeated multiplication:

\[ a\^n=`\underbrace{a\cdot a\cdots a}`{=tex}\_{n`\text{ copies}`{=tex}}
\]

This is similar to repeated addition:

\[
n`\cdot `{=tex}a=`\underbrace{a+a+\cdots+a}`{=tex}\_{n`\text{ copies}`{=tex}}
\]

### Examples

\[ 3\^1=3 \]

\[ 3\^2=3`\cdot3`{=tex}=9 \]

\[ 3\^3=3`\cdot3`{=tex}`\cdot3`{=tex}=27 \]

\[ 3\^0=1 \]

\[ 3\^{-1}=`\frac{1}{3^1}`{=tex}=`\frac13`{=tex} \]

\[ 3\^{-2}=`\frac{1}{3^2}`{=tex}=`\frac19`{=tex} \]

## Page 2 --- Rules of Exponents

# Rules of Exponents

1.  Product rule:

\[ a^n`\cdot `{=tex}a^m=a\^{n+m} \]

2.  Quotient rule:

\[ `\frac{a^n}{a^m}`{=tex}=a\^{n-m} \]

3.  Power of a power:

\[ (a^n)^m=a\^{n`\cdot `{=tex}m} \]

4.  Power of a product:

\[ (a`\cdot `{=tex}b)^n=a^n`\cdot `{=tex}b\^n \]

5.  Power of a quotient:

\[
`\left`{=tex}(`\frac{a}{b}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^n=`\frac{a^n}{b^n}`{=tex}
\]

### Example --- Expand & Simplify

\[ `\left`{=tex}(`\frac{2r^2}{s}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^3 `\cdot`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(`\frac{rs}{t}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^2 \]

## Page 3 --- Radicals

# Radicals

If (a) is a real number, then

\[ `\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex} \]

is a number such that

\[ `\left`{=tex}(`\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^n=a. \]

(`\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex}) may not always exist.

Example:

\[ `\sqrt[2]{-4}`{=tex} \]

In cases where there is more than one choice of value for
(`\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex}), there will be a pair of possible values, one
positive and one negative. In this case, we choose the positive value.

Example:

\[ `\sqrt[2]{4}`{=tex}=`\sqrt4`{=tex} \]

If (n) is even, (`\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex}) is defined only when

\[ a`\ge0`{=tex} \]

and is chosen to be nonnegative.

If (n) is odd, (`\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex}) is defined for all real numbers
(a), and has the same sign as (a) (unique value).

## Page 4 --- Radicals as Fractional Exponents

We may interpret radicals as fractional exponents:

\[ `\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex}=a\^{1/n}. \]

### Examples

\[ `\sqrt[3]{x}`{=tex}=x\^{1/3} \]

\[ `\sqrt{\frac13}`{=tex} =
`\left`{=tex}(`\frac13`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^{1/2} = 3\^{-1/2} \]

\[ `\sqrt[5]{2^2}`{=tex} = (2^2)^{1/5} = 2\^{2/5} \]

Using the rules of exponents, we get similar rules for radicals:

\[ `\sqrt[n]{ab}`{=tex} = `\sqrt[n]{a}`{=tex}`\sqrt[n]{b}`{=tex} \]

\[ `\sqrt[n]{\frac{a}{b}}`{=tex} =
`\frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}}`{=tex} \]

\[ `\sqrt[n]{\sqrt[m]{a}}`{=tex} = `\sqrt[nm]{a}`{=tex} \]

## Page 5 --- Simplifying Radical Expressions

With radical problems, we are typically trying to:

-   minimize the number of radicals present;
-   reduce the amount of terms / shrink the expression inside the
    radicals;
-   remove certain radicals.

### Example --- Simplify

\[ `\sqrt[3]{16x^3y^8z^4}`{=tex} \]

Rewrite (16) as (2\^4):

\[ `\sqrt[3]{2^4x^3y^8z^4}`{=tex} \]

Separate factors that contain complete cubes:

\[ =2`\sqrt[3]{2x^3y^8z^4}`{=tex} \]

\[ =2x`\sqrt[3]{2y^8z^4}`{=tex} \]

\[ =2xy\^2`\sqrt[3]{2y^2z^4}`{=tex} \]

\[ =2xy\^2z`\sqrt[3]{2y^2z}`{=tex} \]

The notes also show the same simplification using fractional exponents:

\[ (16x^3y^8z^4)^{1/3} \]

\[ =16^{1/3}x^1y^{8/3}z^{4/3} \]

and then splitting the improper fractional exponents into integer and
fractional parts to obtain the same simplified radical form.

## Page 6 --- Rationalizing the Denominator

### Example

\[ `\sqrt[5]{\frac{x}{y^2}}`{=tex} \]

The goal is to **rationalize the denominator**.

First separate the quotient:

\[ `\sqrt[5]{\frac{x}{y^2}}`{=tex} =
