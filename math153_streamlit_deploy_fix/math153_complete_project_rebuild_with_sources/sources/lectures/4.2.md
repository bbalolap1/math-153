# 4.2 --- Polynomials of Degree Greater Than 2

## Intermediate Value Theorem for Polynomial Functions

If (f) is a polynomial function and

\[ f(a)`\ne `{=tex}f(b) \]

for some (a\<b), then (f) takes on every value between (f(a)) and (f(b))
on the interval (\[a,b\]).

### Practical Version Used in the Notes

If (f) is a polynomial function and (f(a)) and (f(b)) have **opposite
signs** for some (a\<b), then (f) has a root in the interval

\[ (a,b). \]

## Example --- Showing a Root Exists

Show that

\[ f(x)=x^5+2x^4-6x\^3+2x-3 \]

has a root between (x=1) and (x=2).

At (x=1),

\[ f(1)=1^5+2(1)^4-6(1)\^3+2(1)-3 \]

and the notes obtain

\[ `\boxed{f(1)=-4}`{=tex}. \]

At (x=2),

\[ f(2)=2^5+2(2)^4-6(2)\^3+2(2)-3 \]

and the notes obtain

\[ `\boxed{f(2)=17}`{=tex}. \]

Because (f(x)) is negative at (x=1) and positive at (x=2), the
Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees a root between (1) and (2):

\[ `\boxed{\text{a root exists in }(1,2)}`{=tex}. \]

The notes also illustrate the more general IVT idea with a version of
the polynomial for which

\[ f(1)=-1,`\qquad `{=tex}f(2)=20. \]

Since (3) lies between (-1) and (20), there exists some
(x`\in`{=tex}(1,2)) such that

\[ f(x)=3. \]

# Graphing Polynomials

## Example 1

Let

\[ f(x)=x^3+x^2-4x-4. \]

Find the intervals on which (f(x)) is positive and negative,
respectively. Then graph

\[ y=f(x). \]

Factor:

\[ x^3+x^2-4x-4 = `\boxed{(x-2)(x+2)(x+1)}`{=tex}. \]

Roots:

\[ `\boxed{x=-2,-1,2}`{=tex}. \]

These roots divide the number line into

\[
(-`\infty`{=tex},-2),`\quad`{=tex}(-2,-1),`\quad`{=tex}(-1,2),`\quad`{=tex}(2,`\infty`{=tex}).
\]

### Sign Table

  Interval                   (x-2)   (x+2)   (x+1)   (f(x))
  ------------------------ ------- ------- ------- --------
  ((-`\infty`{=tex},-2))         −       −       −        −
  ((-2,-1))                      −      \+       −       \+
  ((-1,2))                       −      \+      \+        −
  ((2,`\infty`{=tex}))          \+      \+      \+       \+

Therefore:

\[ f(x)\>0`\text{ on }`{=tex}(-2,-1)`\cup`{=tex}(2,`\infty`{=tex}), \]

and

\[ f(x)\<0`\text{ on }`{=tex}(-`\infty`{=tex},-2)`\cup`{=tex}(-1,2). \]

### Graph Information from Pages 4--5

The hand-drawn graph crosses the (x)-axis at

\[ -2,,-1,,2, \]

with cubic end behavior: down on the left and up on the right.

\[ y=x^3+x^2-4x-4, \]

confirming the same three (x)-intercepts and overall shape.

## Example 2

Let

\[ f(x)=x^4-4x^3+3x\^2. \]

Find the intervals on which (f(x)) is positive and negative,
respectively. Then graph

\[ y=f(x). \]

Factor:

\[ x^4-4x^3+3x\^2 = `\boxed{x^2(x-1)(x-3)}`{=tex}. \]

Roots:

\[ `\boxed{x=0,1,3}`{=tex}. \]

The root (x=0) comes from the factor (x\^2), so it has even
multiplicity.

Intervals:

\[
(-`\infty`{=tex},0),`\quad`{=tex}(0,1),`\quad`{=tex}(1,3),`\quad`{=tex}(3,`\infty`{=tex}).
\]

### Sign Table

  Interval                  (x\^2)   (x-1)   (x-3)   (f(x))
  ----------------------- -------- ------- ------- --------
  ((-`\infty`{=tex},0))         \+       −       −       \+
  ((0,1))                       \+       −       −       \+
  ((1,3))                       \+      \+       −        −
  ((3,`\infty`{=tex}))          \+      \+      \+       \+

Thus

\[
f(x)\>0`\text{ on }`{=tex}(-`\infty`{=tex},0)`\cup`{=tex}(0,1)`\cup`{=tex}(3,`\infty`{=tex})
\]

and

\[ f(x)\<0`\text{ on }`{=tex}(1,3). \]

### Graph Information from Pages 7--8

The graph touches the (x)-axis at (x=0), rather than crossing there,
because (x=0) is a double root. It crosses at (x=1) and (x=3). Both ends
rise because the polynomial has even degree with positive leading
coefficient.

Page 8 contains textbook **Figure 10** for

\[ y=x^4-4x^3+3x\^2, \]

showing the same behavior.
