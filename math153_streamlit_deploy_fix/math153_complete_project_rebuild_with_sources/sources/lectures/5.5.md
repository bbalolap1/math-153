# 5.5 --- Properties of Logarithms

For appropriate positive inputs, the notes give three main logarithm
properties.

## 1. Product Rule

\[ `\boxed{\log_a(xy)=\log_a(x)+\log_a(y)}`{=tex} \]

## 2. Quotient Rule

\[ `\boxed{
\log_a\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)
=
\log_a(x)-\log_a(y)
}`{=tex} \]

## 3. Power Rule

\[ `\boxed{
\log_a(x^n)=n\log_a(x)
}`{=tex} \]

The notes specify

\[ x,y\>0 \]

and real values of (n).

## Important Non-Rules

In general,

\[ `\boxed{\log_a(x+y)\ne\log_a(x)+\log_a(y)}`{=tex} \]

and

\[ `\boxed{\log_a(x-y)\ne\log_a(x)-\log_a(y)}`{=tex}. \]

\[ `\log`{=tex}\_a(x\^3`\sqrt `{=tex}y)-`\log`{=tex}\_a(z\^2). \]

# Graph Transformation Example

What is the relationship between

\[ y=`\log`{=tex}\_3(x) \]

and

\[ y=`\log`{=tex}\_3(81x)? \]

Use the product rule:

\[ `\log`{=tex}\_3(81x) = `\log`{=tex}\_3(81)+`\log`{=tex}\_3(x). \]

Since

\[ 81=3\^4, \]

\[ `\log`{=tex}\_3(81)=4. \]

Therefore

\[ `\boxed{
\log_3(81x)=4+\log_3(x)
}`{=tex}. \]

So the graph of (y=`\log`{=tex}\_3(81x)) is the graph of
(y=`\log`{=tex}\_3(x)) shifted **up 4 units**.

\[ (1,0) \]
