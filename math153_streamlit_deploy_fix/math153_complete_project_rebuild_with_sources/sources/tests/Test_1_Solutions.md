## Bonus

For \[ ax\^2+bx+c=0 \] with real (a,b,c), the quadratic formula gives \[
x=`\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}`{=tex}. \]

A non-real root occurs when \[ b\^2-4ac\<0. \]

Then the square root is purely imaginary. If \[
`\sqrt{b^2-4ac}`{=tex}=di \] for some nonzero real (d), the two roots
are \[ `\frac{-b+di}{2a}`{=tex}
`\quad`{=tex}`\text{and}`{=tex}`\quad`{=tex} `\frac{-b-di}{2a}`{=tex}.
\]

They are distinct complex conjugates. Therefore a real-coefficient
quadratic cannot have only one unique non-real root.

\[ `\boxed{\text{Non-real roots occur as a conjugate pair.}}`{=tex} \]
