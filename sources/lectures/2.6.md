## Page 1 --- Inequalities

Inequalities are like equations: they are statements describing a
relation between two quantities, but the equals sign is replaced by one
of

\[ \<, \>, `\le`{=tex}, `\ge`{=tex}. \]

Inequalities have solutions just like equations.

### Example

\[ 2x+3\>11 \]

Test values:

For (x=3):

\[ 2(3)+3=9 \]

and

\[ 9\>11 \]

is false.

For (x=4):

\[ 2(4)+3=11 \]

and

\[ 11\>11 \]

is false.

For (x=5):

\[ 2(5)+3=13 \]

and

\[ 13\>11 \]

is true.

## Page 3 --- Solving Linear and Compound Inequalities

### Example

\[ 4x-3\<2x+5 \]

### Method 1

\[ 4x-2x\<5+3 \]

\[ 2x\<8 \]

\[ x\<4 \]

Interval notation:

\[ `\boxed{(-\infty,4)}`{=tex} \]

### Method 2

\[ -3-5\<2x-4x \]

\[ -8\<-2x \]

Dividing by a negative number flips the inequality:

\[ 4\>x \]

which is again

\[ x\<4. \]

### Continued / Compound Inequality

\[ -6\<2x-4`\le2`{=tex} \]

This is a conjunction:

\[ -6\<2x-4 \]

and

\[ 2x-4`\le2`{=tex}. \]

Solve simultaneously:

\[ -6+4\<2x`\le2`{=tex}+4 \]

\[ -2\<2x`\le6`{=tex} \]

\[ -1\<x`\le3`{=tex} \]

Thus:

\[ `\boxed{(-1,3]}`{=tex} \]

The notes also express this as the intersection

\[ (-1,`\infty`{=tex})`\cap`{=tex}(-`\infty`{=tex},3\]. \]

## Pages 4--5 --- Rational Inequality

### Example

\[ `\frac{-2}{4-3x}`{=tex}\>0 \]

Domain restriction:

\[ 4-3x`\ne0`{=tex} \]

so

\[ `\boxed{x\ne\frac43}`{=tex}. \]

For a quotient to be positive, numerator and denominator must have the
same sign.

The numerator (-2) is negative, so the denominator must also be
negative:

\[ 4-3x\<0 \]

\[ -3x\<-4 \]

Dividing by (-3) flips the inequality:

\[ x\>`\frac43`{=tex}. \]

Therefore:

\[ `\boxed{\left(\frac43,\infty\right)}`{=tex} \]

The notes summarize the sign logic:

\[ `\frac `{=tex}ab\>0 \]

when either

\[ a\>0`\text{ and }`{=tex}b\>0 \]

or

\[ a\<0`\text{ and }`{=tex}b\<0. \]

## Page 4 --- Absolute Value Inequality

### Example

\[ \|x\|\<3 \]

This means (x) is less than (3) units away from (0):

\[ -3\<x\<3. \]

Therefore:

\[ `\boxed{x\in(-3,3)}`{=tex} \]

## Page 6 --- Absolute Value Greater Than

### Example

\[ \|2x+3\|\>9 \]

The expression (2x+3) is at a distance greater than (9) from (0).

This produces a **disjunction**:

\[ 2x+3\<-9 \]

or

\[ 2x+3\>9. \]

First branch:

\[ 2x\<-12 \]

\[ x\<-6. \]

Second branch:

\[ 2x\>6 \]

\[ x\>3. \]

Final answer:

\[ `\boxed{(-\infty,-6)\cup(3,\infty)}`{=tex} \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 7 --- Reciprocal Absolute Value Inequality

### Example

\[ `\frac{2}{|2x+3|}`{=tex}`\ge5`{=tex} \]

Domain restriction:

\[ 2x+3`\ne0`{=tex} \]

so

\[ `\boxed{x\ne-\frac32}`{=tex}. \]

Within the domain,

\[ \|2x+3\|\>0, \]

so multiply both sides by (\|2x+3\|):

\[ 2`\ge5`{=tex}\|2x+3\|. \]

Divide by (5):

\[ \|2x+3\|`\le`{=tex}`\frac25`{=tex}. \]

Convert to a compound inequality:

\[ -`\frac25`{=tex}`\le`{=tex}2x+3`\le`{=tex}`\frac25`{=tex}. \]

Subtract (3):

\[ -`\frac{17}{5}`{=tex}`\le`{=tex}2x`\le`{=tex}-`\frac{13}{5}`{=tex}.
\]

Divide by (2):

\[ -`\frac{17}{10}`{=tex}`\le `{=tex}x`\le`{=tex}-`\frac{13}{10}`{=tex}.
\]

The notes box:

\[ `\boxed{\left[-\frac{17}{10},-\frac{13}{10}\right]}`{=tex} \]

with the earlier domain restriction (x`\ne`{=tex}-`\frac32`{=tex}) also
noted on the page.
