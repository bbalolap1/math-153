# 3.3 --- Lines

## Page 1 --- Slope

If a line (l) is not vertical, its slope is well-defined. For distinct
points \[ P_1(x_1,y_1),`\qquad `{=tex}P_2(x_2,y_2), \] \[
`\boxed{m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\frac{y_1-y_2}{x_1-x_2}}`{=tex}. \]

The notes explicitly warn that \[ `\frac{y_1-y_2}{x_2-x_1}`{=tex} \] is
not the same formula.

## Page 2 --- Slope Examples

For (A(-1,4)), (B(3,2)): \[
m=`\frac{2-4}{3-(-1)}`{=tex}=-`\frac12`{=tex}. \]

For (B(-2,-1)), (A(2,5)): \[
m=`\frac{5-(-1)}{2-(-2)}`{=tex}=`\frac32`{=tex}. \]

For horizontal points (B(-2,3)), (A(4,3)): \[ m=0. \]

For the vertical line through (A(4,-1)), (B(4,4)), slope is undefined
because the denominator is zero.

## Page 3 --- Parallel to the Axes

Through \[ A(-3,4): \]

Parallel to the (x)-axis: \[ `\boxed{y=4}`{=tex}. \]

Parallel to the (y)-axis: \[ `\boxed{x=-3}`{=tex}. \]

## Page 4 --- Point-Slope Form

If a nonvertical line has slope (m) and passes through (P_1(x_1,y_1)),
\[ `\boxed{y-y_1=m(x-x_1)}`{=tex}. \]

Here (P(x,y)) is a general point on the line.

## Pages 5--6 --- Line Through Two Points

Find the line through \[ A(1,7),`\qquad `{=tex}B(-3,2). \]

Slope: \[ m=`\frac{2-7}{-3-1}`{=tex}=`\frac54`{=tex}. \]

Using (A(1,7)): \[ `\boxed{y-7=\frac54(x-1)}`{=tex}. \]

Using (B(-3,2)): \[ `\boxed{y-2=\frac54(x+3)}`{=tex}. \]

Both equations represent the same line.

## Page 7 --- Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Two lines are parallel iff they have the same slope.

Two lines are perpendicular iff their slopes are negative reciprocals:
\[ `\boxed{m_1m_2=-1}`{=tex}. \]

Example slopes: \[ m_1=3,`\qquad `{=tex}m_2=-`\frac13`{=tex} \] and \[
3`\left`{=tex}(-`\frac13`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})=-1. \]

## Page 8 --- Parallel Line

Find a line parallel to \[ 6x+3y=4 \] through \[ P(5,-7). \]

The original line has slope \[ m=-2. \]

Point-slope form: \[ `\boxed{y+7=-2(x-5)}`{=tex}. \]

## Page 9 --- Perpendicular Line

The original slope is \[ m_1=-2. \]

For a perpendicular line: \[ m_1m_2=-1 \] so \[ -2m_2=-1 \] and \[
m_2=`\frac12`{=tex}. \]

Through (P(5,-7)): \[ `\boxed{y+7=\frac12(x-5)}`{=tex}. \]
