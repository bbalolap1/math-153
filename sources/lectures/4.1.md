# 4.1 --- Properties of Division

## Polynomial Degrees

Examples from the notes:

1.  (-5x^4+3x^3-x) --- **4th degree**
2.  (x^2+2x^3-1) --- **3rd degree**
3.  (5-4x) --- **1st degree**
4.  (6x\^0=6) --- **0 degree**, a constant polynomial
5.  The zero polynomial (0) has **no well-defined order/degree**.

## Division Algorithm for Polynomials

If (f(x)) and (p(x)) are polynomials, there exist unique polynomials
(q(x)) and (r(x)) such that

\[ f(x)=p(x)q(x)+r(x), \]

where (q(x)) is the **quotient** and (r(x)) is the **remainder**, under
the condition that (r(x)) is either the zero polynomial or

\[ `\deg`{=tex}(r)\<`\deg`{=tex}(p). \]

This is compared in the notes with whole-number long division:

\[ 3812=6(635)+2. \]

## Polynomial Long Division Example

Find the quotient and remainder when

\[ x\^4-16 \]

is divided by

\[ x\^2-3x+8. \]

The notes obtain

\[ `\boxed{x^4-16=(x^2-3x+8)(x^2+3x+1)+(-21x-24)}`{=tex}. \]

So the quotient is

\[ `\boxed{x^2+3x+1}`{=tex} \]

and the remainder is

\[ `\boxed{-21x-24}`{=tex}. \]

## When the Divisor Has Larger Degree

The notes compare this with

\[ 6=3812(0)+6. \]

Likewise, when (x\^2-3x+8) is divided by (x\^4-16),

\[ x^2-3x+8=(x^4-16)(0)+(x\^2-3x+8). \]

Thus the quotient is (0) and the remainder is the original dividend.

## Exact Division Example

Find the quotient and remainder when

\[ x^3-x^2+x-1 \]

is divided by

\[ x\^2+1. \]

The notes give

\[ `\boxed{x^3-x^2+x-1=(x^2+1)(x-1)+0}`{=tex}. \]

## Linear Divisor Example

Find the quotient and remainder when

\[ 2x\^2-x-6 \]

is divided by

\[ x-2. \]

The notes obtain

\[ `\boxed{2x^2-x-6=(x-2)(2x+3)}`{=tex}. \]

## Division by (x-c)

For division by the first-degree polynomial (x-c),

\[ f(x)=(x-c)q(x)+r(x). \]

Since the remainder must have degree (0), write it as (r_0):

\[ f(x)=(x-c)q(x)+r_0. \]

Set (x=c):

\[ f(c)=(c-c)q(c)+r_0, \]

so

\[ `\boxed{f(c)=r_0}`{=tex}. \]

Therefore

\[ `\boxed{f(x)=(x-c)q(x)+f(c)}`{=tex}. \]

## Remainder Example

Find the remainder when

\[ f(x)=2x^3-x^2+5x+1 \]

is divided by (x-3).

Use

\[ f(3)=2(3)^3-(3)^2+5(3)+1. \]

The notes obtain

\[ `\boxed{f(3)=61}`{=tex}. \]

So the remainder is (61).

## Factor / Root Relationship

The notes state:

> \(c\) is a root of (f(x)) if and only if (x-c) divides (f(x)).

Example:

Find the remainder when

\[ 2x\^2+7x-4 \]

is divided by (x+4=x-(-4)).

\[ f(-4)=2(-4)\^2+7(-4)-4=0. \]

Therefore (x+4) divides the polynomial exactly.

## Building a Polynomial from Roots

Find a polynomial of degree (3) with roots

\[ 2,,-1,,3. \]

The notes construct

\[ f(x)=A(x-2)(x+1)(x-3). \]

Choosing (A=1),

\[ `\boxed{f(x)=(x-2)(x+1)(x-3)}`{=tex}. \]

The expanded form written in the notes is

\[ `\boxed{f(x)=x^3-4x^2+x+6}`{=tex}. \]

## Polynomial with a Given Value

Find a polynomial of degree (2) with roots (-1,2) satisfying

\[ f(3)=12. \]

Start with

\[ f(x)=A(x+1)(x-2). \]

Use (x=3):

\[ 12=A(3+1)(3-2)=4A. \]

Thus

\[ A=3, \]

and

\[ `\boxed{f(x)=3(x+1)(x-2)}`{=tex}. \]
