## Page 1 --- Higher-Degree Inequalities

Most inequalities seen so far, even those involving rational
expressions, have been essentially **linear**, possibly with absolute
values.

The notes now ask:

> What if we consider inequalities involving rational expressions of
> higher degree?

Start with a polynomial example:

\[ 2x\^2-x\<3 \]

Move everything to one side:

\[ 2x\^2-x-3\<0 \]

Factor:

\[ 2x\^2-3x+2x-3\<0 \]

\[ x(2x-3)+1(2x-3)\<0 \]

\[ `\boxed{(x+1)(2x-3)<0}`{=tex} \]

The roots are

\[ x=-1,`\qquad `{=tex}x=`\frac32`{=tex}. \]

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 2 --- Sign Chart

We want

\[ (x+1)(2x-3) \]

to be negative.

Critical points:

\[ x=-1,`\qquad `{=tex}x=`\frac32`{=tex}. \]

These divide the number line into

\[ (-`\infty`{=tex},-1),`\qquad`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(-1,`\frac32`{=tex}`\right`{=tex}),`\qquad`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(`\frac32`{=tex},`\infty`{=tex}`\right`{=tex}). \]

The sign chart in the notes gives:

  Interval                               (x+1)   (2x-3)   Product
  ------------------------------------ ------- -------- ---------
  ((-`\infty`{=tex},-1))                   (-)      (-)       (+)
  ((-1,`\frac32`{=tex}))                   (+)      (-)       (-)
  ((`\frac32`{=tex},`\infty`{=tex}))       (+)      (+)       (+)

Since the original inequality is strict,

\[ (x+1)(2x-3)\<0, \]

the roots are not included.

Therefore:

\[ `\boxed{\left(-1,\frac32\right)}`{=tex} \]

### Non-strict version

For

\[ 2x\^2-x`\le3`{=tex}, \]

we get

\[ (x+1)(2x-3)`\le0`{=tex}. \]

Now the roots are included:

\[ `\left`{=tex}(-1,`\frac32`{=tex}`\right`{=tex}) `\cup`{=tex}{-1}
`\cup`{=tex}`\left`{=tex}{`\frac32`{=tex}`\right`{=tex}} =
`\boxed{\left[-1,\frac32\right]}`{=tex}. \]

## Page 3 --- Rational Inequality with Multiple Critical Points

### Example

\[ `\frac{(2x+1)^2(x-1)}`{=tex} {x(x\^2-1)}\>0 \]

Factor:

\[ x\^2-1=(x-1)(x+1). \]

Thus:

\[ `\frac{(2x+1)^2(x-1)}`{=tex} {x(x-1)(x+1)}\>0. \]

Critical values come from zeros of numerator factors and denominator
factors:

\[
x=-`\frac12`{=tex},`\quad `{=tex}x=1,`\quad `{=tex}x=0,`\quad `{=tex}x=-1.
\]

Domain exclusions are:

\[ `\boxed{x\ne0,\ 1,\ -1}`{=tex}. \]

The notes point out that, as long as

\[ x`\ne1`{=tex}, \]

the common factor (x-1) can be cancelled:

\[ `\frac{(2x+1)^2}{x(x+1)}`{=tex}\>0. \]

Even after cancellation, (x=1) remains excluded from the domain of the
**original** expression.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 4 --- Sign Chart and Strict-Inequality Answer

Critical points split the line into:

\[ (-`\infty`{=tex},-1),`\quad`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(-1,-`\frac12`{=tex}`\right`{=tex}),`\quad`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(-`\frac12`{=tex},0`\right`{=tex}),`\quad`{=tex}
(0,1),`\quad`{=tex} (1,`\infty`{=tex}). \]

For

\[ `\frac{(2x+1)^2}{x(x+1)}`{=tex}\>0, \]

the sign chart gives the positive intervals:

\[ (-`\infty`{=tex},-1), `\qquad`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(-`\frac12`{=tex},0`\right`{=tex}), `\qquad`{=tex} (0,1),
`\qquad`{=tex} (1,`\infty`{=tex}). \]

The notes box:

\[ `\boxed{
(-\infty,-1)
\cup
\left(-\frac12,0\right)
\cup
(0,1)
\cup
(1,\infty)
}`{=tex} \]

The points (x=-1,0,1) are excluded because they make the original
denominator zero.

The point

\[ x=-`\frac12`{=tex} \]

makes the numerator zero, so it is excluded for the strict inequality
(\>0).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Page 4 --- Non-strict Version

The notes then consider

\[ `\frac{(2x+1)^2(x-1)}`{=tex} {x(x\^2-1)}`\ge0`{=tex}. \]

Question:

> Where is numerator (0)?\
> (denominator not (0))

The valid numerator root is

\[ x=-`\frac12`{=tex}. \]

So (x=-`\frac12`{=tex}) can now be included.

The boxed solution is:

\[ `\boxed{
(-\infty,-1)
\cup
\left[-\frac12,0\right)
\cup
(0,1)
\cup
(1,\infty)
}`{=tex} \]

The denominator roots

\[ -1, 0, 1 \]

remain excluded.
