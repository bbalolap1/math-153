# 5.3 - Natural Exponential Function

## The Number (e)

The notes begin with repeated compounding:

\[ `\left`{=tex}(1+`\frac12`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^2,`\qquad`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(1+`\frac13`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^3,`\qquad`{=tex}
`\left`{=tex}(1+`\frac14`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^4,`\ldots`{=tex} \]

As the number of compounding periods increases,

\[ `\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n=e}`{=tex} \]

where

\[ `\boxed{e\approx2.71828\ldots}`{=tex}. \]

More generally,

\[ `\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac rn\right)^{nt}=e^{rt}}`{=tex}.
\]

The notes describe the left side as discrete compounding and (e\^{rt})
as continuous compounding.

## Compound Interest Example

Suppose \$1,000 is invested for one year at (9%).

\[ P=1000,`\qquad `{=tex}r=0.09,`\qquad `{=tex}t=1. \]

### Quarterly

\[ A=1000`\left`{=tex}(1+`\frac{0.09}{4}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^4
`\approx1093.0833`{=tex}. \]

### Monthly

\[ A=1000`\left`{=tex}(1+`\frac{0.09}{12}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^{12}
`\approx1093.8069`{=tex}. \]

### Weekly

\[ A=1000`\left`{=tex}(1+`\frac{0.09}{52}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^{52}
`\approx1094.0892`{=tex}. \]

### Continuously

\[ A=1000e\^{0.09} `\approx1094.1742`{=tex}. \]

## Graphing a Natural Exponential Expression

Sketch

\[ `\boxed{f(x)=\frac{e^x+e^{-x}}2}`{=tex}. \]

The table in the notes gives approximately:

     \(x\)    0.0    0.5    1.0    1.5    2.0
  -------- ------ ------ ------ ------ ------
    (f(x))   1.00   1.13   1.54   2.35   3.76

The notes check symmetry:

\[ f(-x)=`\frac{e^{-x}+e^x}{2}`{=tex}=f(x). \]

Therefore the function is even and its graph is symmetric about the
(y)-axis.
